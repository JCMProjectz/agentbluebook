# Fresh Venture List — New Niches (Not LLC Extensions)

**Date:** September 2026
**Constraint from the operator:** completely different niches/categories than Michigan LLC Service; state expansion of the LLC service proceeds separately. Apply the original criteria (high traffic, high conversion, MRR preferred, ~fully automatable, high ceiling, passive) plus what the Michigan LLC data proved.

## What the Michigan LLC data proved (the pattern to replicate elsewhere)

- 2 paying customers in one week at month ~2, $0 ad spend, pure organic intent traffic.
- The conversion formula: **mandatory or deadline-driven process + government-adjacent confusion + localized trust ("feels local, not corporate") + transparent fixed price + transactional purchase (no sales relationship) + machine fulfillment**.
- Localized beats national for state/city-scoped processes; fragmented local rules are a *moat* because national players do them thinly.

Each idea below is scored against that formula. Positives and negatives are stated for every one — including deal-breakers.

---

## 1. Rental Compliance Autopilot (landlord city-compliance deadlines) — best MRR fit

**What:** Track and manage city rental registrations, certificate-of-compliance renewals, and inspection deadlines for small landlords; generate and pre-fill the filings; remind, escalate, file where portals allow. Priced per property (~$49–79/property/yr or $7–10/mo) — landlords own multiple properties, so ARPU stacks.

**Key findings (facts):**
- Detroit requires registration + a Certificate of Compliance for every non-owner-occupied rental; certs last 3 years; failure is a blight violation with escalating fines; new owners must re-register within 90 days ([detroitmi.gov](https://detroitmi.gov/departments/buildings-safety-engineering-and-environmental-department-bseed/bseed-divisions/property-maintenance/tenant-rental-property/landlord-rental), [Municode](https://library.municode.com/mi/detroit/codes/code_of_ordinances/304930?nodeId=PTIIICICO_CH9BUBURE_ARTIDEPRMACO_DIV3REREPR_SDAINGE)).
- Grand Rapids requires annual registration plus certification on 2/4/6-year cycles ([grandrapidsmi.gov](https://www.grandrapidsmi.gov/departments/code-compliance/rental-properties/rental-certification-program/)).
- Every mid-size city has its own variant — exactly the fragmentation that kept national players out of state LLC long-tail.
- Private inspection firms already monetize this pain ([The Inspection Group](https://www.theinspectiongroup.com/detroit-rental-property-compliance/)) — but nobody productizes the *deadline/filing layer* cheaply.

**Positives:** true recurring revenue tied to recurring legal obligations; multi-property customers; localized trust angle is the product; deadline+fine urgency = painkiller; pipeline reuse ~80% (city rule pages = SEO, filings = n8n+Claude); landlords are findable (assessor records are public → warm outreach that isn't scammy).
**Negatives:** per-city rule ingestion is a grind (each city = research); the inspection itself is physical — we manage paper and dates, not the visit; enforcement laxity in some cities weakens urgency; per-city TAM is modest (thousands, not millions, of landlords) so ceiling comes from city count; some cities' filings are paper/PDF-only (fine) or in-person (bad).

## 2. Executor Autopilot / "After-Loss OS" — biggest pain, highest willingness to pay

**What:** For the person settling a deceased family member's affairs: state-specific step-by-step plan, every notification letter and form generated (SSA, banks, credit bureaus, DMV, insurers, subscriptions), probate deadline tracking by county, document vault. $199 flat or $29/mo during settlement (estates take 6–18 months → quasi-MRR).

**Key findings (facts):**
- ~3.1M US deaths/yr; DIY (pro se) probate is legal in most states for straightforward estates ([AllLaw/Nolo](https://www.alllaw.com/articles/nolo/wills-trusts/executor-need-hire-lawyer.html)).
- Empathy raised $13M → $30M → [$47M Series B](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/bk0xfd3tp) for exactly this job — but sells **B2B2C via insurers/employers** (35M policyholders), leaving the *direct-to-consumer, pay-once* end of the market thin.
- Free tools ([executor.org](https://executor.org/blog/online-executor-tools/)) prove organic demand; the money competitor at the direct level is basically "hire a probate lawyer for $3–7K."
- Search intent ("what to do when someone dies in [state]", "probate in [state] without a lawyer") is evergreen, localized, and served today by law-firm content farms.

**Positives:** maximal confusion+deadline+aversion pattern; willingness to pay is high and price-insensitive at $199 vs $5K lawyers; state/county localization is a real moat; Claude is genuinely good at the fulfillment (checklists, letters, plain-English probate rules); immediate-intent buyers, zero relationship needed.
**Negatives:** UPL line must be managed carefully (information + document prep, never advice — same guardrails as LLC filings but higher stakes); marketing must be tasteful (SEO around death converts but must never feel predatory); grieving users may email — support isn't quite zero; county-level content depth is a real build; one estate = one customer (retention ends when settlement ends).

## 3. Name Change Autopilot (marriage/divorce) — proven market, beatable incumbents, but one-time

**What:** Generate the complete personalized packet to change your name everywhere — SSA, state DMV, passport, IRS, voter, banks — pre-filled, with per-state instructions. $39–59 digital.

**Key findings (facts):**
- Two incumbents prove the market: [NewlyNamed](https://newlynamed.com/blogs/guides/best-name-change-service) ($49 print-at-home / $99 box, 4.9★) and [HitchSwitch](https://blog.hitchswitch.com/2026/01/best-name-change-services-after-marriage-2026/) ($39.99–129.99, 4.5★); both reviewed by [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/name-change-services) and comparison sites — a mature affiliate ecosystem exists.
- ~2M US marriages/yr, most involving a name change; divorce name-restoration is a second, less-served wedge.
- No published market-size data for the niche specifically — sized by proxy only.

**Positives:** demand is proven (not speculative); fulfillment is 100% document generation — the purest Claude+n8n fit on this list; per-state DMV variation rewards localized depth; divorce angle is underserved; could undercut at $39 all-digital.
**Negatives:** **one-time revenue, zero MRR** — fails your stated preference; two entrenched brands own the wedding-media distribution channel (The Knot, brides' checklists); seasonal; print/mail tier adds physical ops if you match the incumbents' box product; winning SERPs means out-ranking the incumbents' own "best services" listicles (they've fortified).

## 4. Website Legal Compliance Suite (Termly model) — pure software MRR, but crowded

**What:** Auto-generated, auto-updated privacy policy / cookie banner / ToS for small websites, $10–20/mo, sold white-label through freelance web designers as the wedge.

**Positives:** 100% software, genuine MRR, national ceiling, regulation churn (state privacy laws) creates ongoing update value and lock-in; white-label-to-designers is a distribution path incumbents underuse.
**Negatives:** heavily crowded (Termly, iubenda, Enzuzo, TermsFeed) with mature SEO and $0–10/mo pricing — the wedge must be distribution, not product; legal-accuracy liability requires careful disclaimers and update discipline; churn tracks small-site mortality; nothing localized about it — you'd be fighting exactly the national game you just said you don't want to play.

## 5. Trade License Exam Prep, per state (contractors, electricians, cosmetology) — content flywheel, one-time

**What:** State-specific exam prep (practice tests, study guides) for licensed trades; $99–199 one-time or $19/mo until you pass. The AgentBluebook content pipeline literally generates the SEO layer; question banks are Claude-generated + human-spot-checked.

**Positives:** fragmented per state+trade (localization moat again); evergreen intent ("michigan builders license exam"); high perceived value vs price; fully digital.
**Negatives:** one-time-ish revenue; **quality liability is real** — if pass rates disappoint, refunds and reputation sink it, and spot-checking question banks against actual state exam blueprints is genuine manual work per state/trade; incumbent per-vertical players (PSI-adjacent prep companies) exist in the big trades; not passive during the build phase.

## 6. STR (Airbnb) Permit Compliance — good pattern, shrinking ground

**What:** Rental Compliance Autopilot's cousin for short-term-rental hosts: city STR permits, renewals, occupancy-tax registrations.
**Positives:** hosts pay readily (permit loss = income loss — strongest urgency on this list); same localized fragmentation moat; overlaps build with #1.
**Negatives:** many cities are actively *shrinking* or banning STRs — building on regulatory sand; per-city grind identical to #1 with a smaller, more volatile customer base; platform (Airbnb) could absorb the job natively any year.

## 7. Small-Business Grant & Incentive Alerts — attractive on paper, weak retention mechanics

**What:** $19–29/mo alerts matching local/state/federal grants to a business's profile; Claude does the matching, scraping does the sourcing.
**Positives:** automatable end-to-end; every small business is a prospect; localized angle works ("Michigan small business grants" has real volume).
**Negatives:** value is lottery-shaped — subscribers who don't win churn fast; the niche has scam-adjacent perception (grant-guru schemes) so trust-building costs are high; sourcing quality is a forever-chore; no deadline/obligation driver — it's a vitamin.

## 8. ADA Accessibility Monitoring — listed to be rejected

**What:** $29–99/mo automated accessibility scans marketed on lawsuit fear.
**Why rejected on the facts:** the category leader accessiBe was ordered by the FTC to pay $1M (Jan 2025) over misleading claims; overlay/scanner products are actively campaigned against by the accessibility community; marketing runs on fear in a taint-heavy niche — same problem as trademark cold outreach, worse. Skip.
